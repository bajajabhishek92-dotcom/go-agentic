# Cars, Not Faster Horses: A Commercial Definition of the Agent Era

Sequoia’s AI Ascent 2026 framing is intentionally non-academic: stop arguing about AGI checklists and notice that **the shape of software work has changed**.

## Communication revolutions vs. computation revolutions

Most founders alive today lived through communication revolutions—internet, cloud, mobile—where the breakthrough was distributing information. AI is framed as a *computation* revolution: how information is processed. The practical difference is that the foundation under your product moves continuously. New capabilities arrive as discontinuous jumps, not only as nicer UX.

Pat Grady highlights three inflection points: ChatGPT (pretraining), reasoning models (inference-time compute), and long-horizon coding agents. The commercial punchline: if you can dispatch an agent that recovers from failure and persists until a job is done, you are—functionally—in an AGI-like regime for work, even without a technical AGI consensus.

## Faster horses vs. cars

Earlier AI apps made people ~10–40% more productive without changing the job. The new generation aims for ~10–40× and reorganizes the work itself. That is the “cars have arrived” metaphor: you do not ride a car the way you ride a horse, and you do not build a car company like a stable.

Konstantine Buhler / Jim Gao’s economic story pairs with this: cognitive work is where physical labor was during industrialization—an enormous pool of economic activity about to be re-factored, with trillions of dollars of value in play between labs (tech-out) and application companies (customer-back).

## Advice implied for builders

- Design for affordance and diffusion gaps: models can do more than Fortune 500 companies have deployed
- Build moats from the customer back (workflows, data, distribution), not from wrapping the latest demo
- Assume the floor keeps moving; architecture that cannot absorb a new model release is a liability
