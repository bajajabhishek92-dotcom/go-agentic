# Applied Research Labs Inside Product Companies

Harvey’s Gabe Pereyra describes building a serious research lab without frontier-lab budgets. The broader Sequoia pattern: **application companies are becoming research orgs** because domain intelligence is the moat.

## What these labs actually do

- Domain evals and benchmarks that public leaderboards miss
- Post-training and tool-use policies for professional workflows (law, coding factories, security)
- Data engines from production traces
- Small teams of researchers paired tightly with product

The constraint is focus: you cannot recreate a full pretraining stack; you *can* out-specialize labs on a vertical. Jerry Tworek and Rohan Anil’s Core Automation discussion adds a cautionary note from the AGI-lab side—scaling RL in the lab does not automatically transfer to messy deployment—so applied labs that own real environments have a structural advantage.

Chai Discovery’s “bitter lesson” for drug design rhymes: treat the scientific workflow as another scaling problem with the right learning setup, not only as artisan chemistry.

### Practice checklist

- Fund evals like product infrastructure
- Hire researchers who will touch customers and data pipelines
- Prefer problems with verifiers (tests, binders, win-rates)
- Publish enough to recruit; keep the data engine proprietary
