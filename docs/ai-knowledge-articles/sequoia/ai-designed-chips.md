# AI That Designs Chips: Closing the Loop on Silicon

Ricursive Intelligence’s founders (Anna Goldie, Azalia Mirhoseini) represent a recursive bet: use AI to design the chips that run AI, informed by earlier Google work on learned placement and the painful gap between academic metrics and real tapeout constraints.

## The lesson from research-to-fab

Academic proxies (e.g., half-perimeter wire length) can look publishable and still anger chip designers who care about timing, power, manufacturability, and tool flows. The product lesson generalizes beyond EDA: **if your reward function is not the real objective, RL will optimize the wrong world**.

Successful AI-for-chips work sits inside the real toolchain, with rewards defined by engineers who ship silicon—not only by papers.

## Why it matters for AI companies

Hardware–software co-design (Dylan Patel / SemiAnalysis themes; NVIDIA’s stack) is a 100× lever. If models help generate better accelerators and placements, the cost curve of intelligence bends again—complementing energy and data-efficiency efforts.
