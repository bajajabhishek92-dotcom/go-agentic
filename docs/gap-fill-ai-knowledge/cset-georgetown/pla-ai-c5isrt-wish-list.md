# Reading the PLA’s AI Wish List (C5ISRT)—Without Hype

Open-source **requests for proposal (RFPs)** are one of the few systematic windows into military AI demand. CSET’s *China’s Military AI Wish List* reviews thousands of Chinese-language PLA RFPs published between 1 January 2023 and 31 December 2024, focused on **C5ISRT**: command, control, communications, computers, cyber, intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, and targeting. The pedagogical goal is **priority mapping**, not alarmist laundry lists. RFPs are demand signals—not proof of fielded mass capability.

## What the RFPs emphasize

Across domains, requests cluster around generating, augmenting, and fusing more data to speed decisions and improve precision. Documented interest includes:

- **AI decision-support systems (AI-DSS)** for strategic uses (including open-source-fed analysis) and tactical uses such as targeting—especially salient where institutions see AI as offsetting perceived **officer-corps experience gaps**.
- Sensor enhancement and **data-fusion** algorithms.
- Maritime and undersea detection oriented toward countering perceived US naval advantages; technologies aimed at countering **space-based** systems.
- Installation security tools (e.g. facial and gait recognition) and information tools including deepfake **generation and detection**—read as procurement interest in surveillance and cognitive-domain capabilities.

Budgets and timelines are often **small and short** (commonly three to six months), consistent with prototyping, experimentation, and outreach to **commercial vendors** beyond the traditional defense industrial base. Documents can be strikingly explicit about sensitive domains; that frankness is itself an analytic clue about acquisition culture and the search for nontraditional suppliers.

## Implications CSET draws for planners (condensed)

- Invest in **counter-sensing, deception, and ISR resilience** where peer sensing improves.
- Prepare to counter emerging Chinese AI-DSS while pursuing **dialogue on responsible use** to reduce miscalculation—even if binding commitments are unlikely.
- Rethink military signaling, deterrence, and crisis management if AI systems increasingly mediate interpretation of open-source global events.
- Pair public awareness and detection tools against expanded information manipulation, including more systematic deepfake use.
- Match peer **rapid experimentation** with acquisition reform and responsible adoption at home.
- Treat advanced hardware access and export-control posture as inputs to the same modernization story.
- Keep **sustained open-source monitoring** to rightsize claims as RFPs evolve.

## Teaching discipline

- Quote **categories and incentives**; avoid graphic how-to detail.
- Pair military demand signals with civil AI industrial policy (chips, embodied AI, open models) for a full picture.
- Separate **ambition, experimentation, and deployment**—RFP volume ≠ readiness.
- Use the corpus to teach method: language skill + procurement literacy + careful inference.

## Connecting the dots across the gap-fill corpus

This article sits in a multi-source teaching set. Cross-link it when you teach:

- **Compute and factories** (Epoch chip geography, NVIDIA DSX/recovery/cooling) when the bottleneck is physical capacity, power, or utilization rather than model cleverness.
- **Agents and harnesses** (Anthropic sandboxes, NVIDIA NeMo failure loops, OpenShell/Sentry) when the bottleneck is tool privilege, traces, or runtime policy.
- **Governance and privacy** (CSET actor maps, FPF ADM/chatbot/biometric drafts, Google contextual agent privacy) when the bottleneck is legal scope, preemption, or appropriate information flow.
- **Science and integrity** (Epoch acknowledgment trends, Spectrum AI-slop, Google Chain-of-Evidence, wet-lab grounding) when the bottleneck is evidence, disclosure, or physical verification.

Assign one cross-folder compare-and-contrast: same concept, two source cultures (lab blog vs policy center vs engineering journalism). Students should leave able to explain *why* the framings differ—not only recite facts from one page.

## Classroom and practice checklist

Use this piece as a concept explainer, not a news brief. When teaching or briefing:

- Restate the **core mechanism** in one sentence before citing any organization or product name.
- Separate **what is measured** from **what is claimed**—numbers, thresholds, and evaluation designs are the durable content.
- Map at least one **failure mode** (silent fallback, overclaim, approval fatigue, distribution shift, or missing evidence chain).
- Name the **operator decision** the idea changes: release gating, procurement, curriculum design, facility build, or legal scope.
- Leave students with a **verification habit**: which primary source, diagnostic, or audit would falsify the headline?

These habits travel across geopolitics, privacy law, compute economics, agent harnesses, and applied science stacks alike.

## Takeaways

- PLA RFPs are a rare **structured demand map** for C5ISRT AI priorities.
- Decision support, sensing/fusion, maritime/space counters, and information tools recur; timelines look experimental.
- Policy responses span resilience, dialogue, signaling redesign, and monitoring—not only matching every line item.
- Analytic humility: explicit wishes are not the same as proven inventory.
