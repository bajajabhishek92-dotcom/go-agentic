# Unpacking the US National Policy Framework for AI

In March 2026 the White House released a **National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence**—legislative recommendations for Congress, not just another strategy memo. CSET’s unpacking piece stresses the genre difference: prior documents (July 2025 AI Action Plan, executive orders) set direction; this framework **names statutes Congress should pass** to match administration goals. It is non-binding, contains no self-executing executive directives, and varies widely in specificity.

## Why it exists: preemption after failed moratoria

The framework follows the December 2025 executive order directing advisors to prepare a **uniform federal AI policy** after Congress repeatedly failed to enact a **state-AI-law moratorium** (including attempts to condition federal funds on non-enforcement). The political project is clear: **preempt fragmented state AI rules** and assert federal leadership aligned with the administration’s agenda. CSET reads the document as an opening move in negotiation with Congress—not a finished regime.

## What the framework signals (and what it leaves open)

Compared with the Action Plan’s agency-heavy focus, the framework mixes innovation/energy priorities with issues that already move statehouses and polls:

- **Child safety / parental empowerment** appears more prominently than in prior Trump-era AI strategy—likely acknowledgment of bipartisan chatbot and youth-protection bills.
- **Censorship / free speech / “ideological bias”** language continues themes from earlier executive orders, including efforts to future-proof content priorities against later administrations.
- **Deepfakes and identity misuse** get commercial and IP framing, with carveouts for parody/satire that will be hard to operationalize; unlike the child-safety section, deepfake discussion does **not** clearly protect existing state laws from preemption.
- **Innovation sandboxes**, federal dataset readiness, and sector-specific existing authorities appear without detailed implementation recipes.
- **Frontier catastrophic risk** is lightly treated—agencies should “understand and plan”—consistent with a light-touch innovation posture.

Section VII’s preemption categories are especially broad: states should not govern (1) AI development, (2) use of AI for activity that would be lawful without AI, and (3) developer liability for unlawful third-party conduct. CSET flags the ambiguity—does (2) block employment/health AI rules, or only prevent AI-specific safety duties?—and notes the administration may prefer Congress to **ban state action without filling the federal gap**.

## Congress as the real arena

Two days before the White House release, Senator Blackburn floated a **TRUMP AI AMERICA Act** discussion draft with overlapping child/creator themes but different preemption design (conflict preemption vs categorical state abstention) and contested extras such as Section 230 sunset and developer duties of care. Alignment is partial; industry pushback and interstate politics will decide what survives. Midterm pressure may favor “low-hanging fruit” such as workforce-task tracking and agency technical capacity—even if preemption remains toxic on both aisles.

## How to teach the document

- Distinguish **strategy docs**, **executive orders**, and **legislative frameworks**.
- Track preemption fights as seriously as “AI safety” substance—they are the US governance drama.
- Read section menus as **bargaining chips**, not enacted law.
- Watch state laboratories (chatbot, deepfake, ADM statutes) for collision with any federal clause that actually passes.

## Classroom and practice checklist

Use this piece as a concept explainer, not a news brief. When teaching or briefing:

- Restate the **core mechanism** in one sentence before citing any organization or product name.
- Separate **what is measured** from **what is claimed**—numbers, thresholds, and evaluation designs are the durable content.
- Map at least one **failure mode** (silent fallback, overclaim, approval fatigue, distribution shift, or missing evidence chain).
- Name the **operator decision** the idea changes: release gating, procurement, curriculum design, facility build, or legal scope.
- Leave students with a **verification habit**: which primary source, diagnostic, or audit would falsify the headline?

These habits travel across geopolitics, privacy law, compute economics, agent harnesses, and applied science stacks alike.

## Takeaways

- The framework is a **call for statutes**, not regulation by memo.
- Preemption is the through-line; child safety and deepfakes are coalition lubricants.
- Ambiguous carveouts will become litigation if Congress copies them loosely.
- Impact equals what Congress enacts—and what courts uphold—not PDF page count.
