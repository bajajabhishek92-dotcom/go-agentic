# Open Models as Soft Power in US–China Competition

Weights that anyone can download are not only a research norm—they are a **geostrategic distribution channel**. CSET/RAND analysis (*Open Models, Soft Power, and the Spectrum of U.S.-China Artificial Intelligence Competition*, March 2026) argues the open-model ecosystem is becoming a primary arena for influence: who sets defaults for developers worldwide may matter as much as who trains the single best closed model. National security remains one dimension; markets, standards, and stack lock-in are others.

## Soft power, defined for AI stacks

Soft power here means shaping others’ preferences through ideology, commercial opportunity, and attractiveness—not only military strength. Broad adoption of one country’s open models can **lock researchers and startups into that country’s tooling, licenses, cloud, and norms**, especially across the Global South where compute budgets and vendor choice are constrained. “Open” is therefore an export strategy as much as a research culture.

## Diverging plan language

The paper contrasts action-plan rhetoric:

- **US AI Action Plan** — Create a supportive environment for open models (compute access, partnerships, SME adoption) so leading open models reflect American values and become commercial/academic standards—while still leaving release choices to developers and tying openness to exporting the wider US stack (hardware, models, software, applications, standards).
- **China’s Global AI Governance Action Plan** — Stresses transnational open-source communities, lower barriers, Global South access, and UN-centered cooperation—while language about avoiding “duplication of investment” also reads as a push to entrench Chinese platforms and standards.

China’s earlier (2017) development plan already treated open AI as an innovation pathway; 2025 releases such as DeepSeek R1 and Moonshot Kimi K2 made highly capable Chinese open weights globally salient. Perception matters: if the US is seen as closed-by-default and China as the sharer, soft power accrues regardless of raw MMLU deltas.

## Policy levers the analysis emphasizes

Authors recommend stronger US support for a healthy open-model ecosystem; **recalibration of export controls** where they conflict with legitimate open diffusion; and incentives for **permissive licensing**. The strategic aim is preventing a world where China is perceived as the primary champion of shared AI benefits while US policy accidentally cedes the developer default layer.

## How to score “open” in competition analysis

- Track **downloads, derivatives, and regional adoption**, not only peak closed-model evals.
- Separate **weights availability** from ecosystem gravity (tooling, docs, hosting, licenses).
- Read governance plans as **stack-export strategies** with openness as marketing and mechanism.
- Watch whether export-control design accidentally starves allied open ecosystems.

## Classroom and practice checklist

Use this piece as a concept explainer, not a news brief. When teaching or briefing:

- Restate the **core mechanism** in one sentence before citing any organization or product name.
- Separate **what is measured** from **what is claimed**—numbers, thresholds, and evaluation designs are the durable content.
- Map at least one **failure mode** (silent fallback, overclaim, approval fatigue, distribution shift, or missing evidence chain).
- Name the **operator decision** the idea changes: release gating, procurement, curriculum design, facility build, or legal scope.
- Leave students with a **verification habit**: which primary source, diagnostic, or audit would falsify the headline?

These habits travel across geopolitics, privacy law, compute economics, agent harnesses, and applied science stacks alike.

## Builder implication

Open weights change **who can fine-tune and redistribute**, not who owns the best closed API on day one. For product strategy, track license terms, data/compute access for downstream builders, and month-scale catch-up on capability indices (see [`../epoch-ai/open-vs-closed-cyber-gap.md`](../epoch-ai/open-vs-closed-cyber-gap.md) and [`../market-players/open-weight-ecosystems-and-soft-power.md`](../market-players/open-weight-ecosystems-and-soft-power.md)). Soft power is an adoption story: ecosystems win when defaults stick.

## Takeaways

- Open releases are soft-power instruments when they set **defaults and lock-in**.
- Compare US and Chinese plans on **openness claims vs platform entrenchment**.
- Soft power fails if weights exist but developers cannot access compute or permissive licenses.
- Competition literacy requires adoption maps alongside leaderboard charts.
