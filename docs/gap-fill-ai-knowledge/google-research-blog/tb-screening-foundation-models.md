# TB Screening: When Foundation Models Meet Frontline Radiology

Tuberculosis remains a top infectious killer—on the order of 1.23 million deaths yearly—despite being preventable and curable. Geography decides access: rural clinics and mining sites may be days from a radiologist. AI that triages chest radiographs (CXRs) for TB signs targets that access gap. The strategic shift is from one-off CAD binaries to **open adaptable foundations**.

## Prove the clinical question first

Can automated readers detect subtle TB signs (apical infiltrates, cavitary lesions) at expert-comparable quality under real prevalence and hardware noise? Independent evaluations such as Stop TB Partnership assessments of computer-aided detection matter more than a single-hospital ROC. Partnerships that deploy readers in minutes (for example Nexus AI–style African clinic rollouts) stress latency, workflow, and CE MDR–class regulatory paths—not only model cards.

## From point solutions to open foundations

A custom model with one partner cannot cover the global burden. Open chest X-ray embeddings and health AI developer foundations (HAI-DEF / MedGemma-class building blocks) let ministries and developers adapt screening to local data instead of waiting for a vendor monolith. “Foundation model” here means **representation reuse for scarce-expert imaging**, with adaptation under distribution shift (new sites, portable devices).

## Deployment knobs that dominate architecture

- Thresholds tuned to local prevalence and follow-up capacity.
- Triage vs sole-reader governance.
- On-device or near-device inference for offline clinics.
- Audit trails and human override for every path.
- Pair with maternal ultrasound lessons: frontline hardware and generalization pilots are the product.

Assign one cross-folder compare-and-contrast: same concept, two source cultures (lab blog vs policy center vs engineering journalism). Students should leave able to explain *why* the framings differ—not only recite facts from one page.

## Common misconceptions to correct early

Students often conflate adjacent ideas that this article carefully separates. Correct these early:

- **Capability ≠ deployment.** A strong score, demo, or RFP does not prove fielded systems, legal compliance, or safe operations.
- **Openness ≠ safety or soft power by itself.** Weights, licenses, orchestration, and compute access each change the influence and risk story differently.
- **Human-in-the-loop ≠ meaningful control** when volume, latency, or rubber-stamping hollow out review.
- **Security transforms ≠ category exits** in privacy law: a one-way template can still identify; a sandbox can still be misconfigured.
- **Foundation models ≠ finished products** in health, Earth, or science: adaptation, thresholds, escalation, and evidence chains remain the product.

Ask learners to spot which misconception a headline is exploiting, then rewrite the headline with the distinction restored.

## Classroom and practice checklist

Use this piece as a concept explainer, not a news brief. When teaching or briefing:

- Restate the **core mechanism** in one sentence before citing any organization or product name.
- Separate **what is measured** from **what is claimed**—numbers, thresholds, and evaluation designs are the durable content.
- Map at least one **failure mode** (silent fallback, overclaim, approval fatigue, distribution shift, or missing evidence chain).
- Name the **operator decision** the idea changes: release gating, procurement, curriculum design, facility build, or legal scope.
- Leave students with a **verification habit**: which primary source, diagnostic, or audit would falsify the headline?

These habits travel across geopolitics, privacy law, compute economics, agent harnesses, and applied science stacks alike.

## Takeaways

- Clinical imaging AI compresses scarce expertise into a verifiable frontline step.
- Independent multi-site evaluation, regulatory fit, and open adaptable foundations outrank leaderboard bragging.
- Prevalence-aware thresholds beat global defaults.
- Foundations exist to be adapted—not frozen as a single global classifier.
