# Nine Big Questions Benchmarks Should Answer

Benchmarks are not trophies. Epoch’s “9 big questions” framing treats them as instruments for **economic and trajectory questions** that decide AI’s impact. If a suite does not help answer at least one of these, it is probably decorative. The essay is a curriculum organizer: invest eval effort where decisions live.

## The question list (compressed)

1. **Can AI do my job?** — Not one bugfix: messy, open-ended roles. Most workplace use still covers *parts* of tasks (Epoch polling). Push examples: MirrorCode (large packages from scratch), Remote Labor Index (freelance deliverables), Andon Café (an agent-run business as a living case study).

2. **Is AI progressing where big economic impact should eventually land?** — After chat and coding “moments,” which domains are next? Benchmarks should track those frontiers early—not only the domains already saturated with leaderboards.

3. **How does capability translate into productivity and surplus?** — Scores ≠ GDP; need bridges from evals to workflow studies and wages.

4. **What bottlenecks remain after raw intelligence rises?** — Tools, verification, organizational process, computer-use reliability, context and permissions.

5. **How fast is the frontier moving—and is it broadening?** — ECI-style aggregates and domain slices (math, cyber, SWE). Pace and breadth are different claims.

6. **Open vs closed / cheap vs expensive capability?** — Time lags and $/threshold curves matter for diffusion and risk. A closed-only story misreads who can act.

7. **Can AI automate AI R&D?** — End-to-end research evals (InnovationEval and peers), not only coding subtasks.

8. **Where do models still fail in ways that block deployment?** — Taste, judgment, long-horizon coherence, safety-critical domains.

9. **What would change forecasts if we measured better?** — Benchmarking as an input to timelines and policy, not a substitute for them.

(Exact wording varies in the source essay; the durable idea is to **organize eval investment around decision-relevant questions**.)

## Philosophy to teach

- Prefer tasks graded against **human professional standards** and real deliverables.
- Expect more **repeated case studies** (agent-run orgs) alongside classical fixed suites.
- Avoid optimizing only for easily verifiable puzzles that models can be trained to game.
- Use indices (ECI) to compare incomplete score matrices; use domain suites to answer domain questions.
- Ask of every new suite: which of the nine does it serve—and which does it pretend to serve?

## Classroom sequence

- Pick a public benchmark; map it to one primary question and name the mismatches.
- Contrast MirrorCode-style package building with multiple-choice science quizzes.
- Design a one-page eval proposal that starts from a decision (“hire or not,” “open weights or not”) rather than from a dataset.

## Connecting the dots across the gap-fill corpus

This article sits in a multi-source teaching set. Cross-link it when you teach:

- **Compute and factories** (Epoch chip geography, NVIDIA DSX/recovery/cooling) when the bottleneck is physical capacity, power, or utilization rather than model cleverness.
- **Agents and harnesses** (Anthropic sandboxes, NVIDIA NeMo failure loops, OpenShell/Sentry) when the bottleneck is tool privilege, traces, or runtime policy.
- **Governance and privacy** (CSET actor maps, FPF ADM/chatbot/biometric drafts, Google contextual agent privacy) when the bottleneck is legal scope, preemption, or appropriate information flow.
- **Science and integrity** (Epoch acknowledgment trends, Spectrum AI-slop, Google Chain-of-Evidence, wet-lab grounding) when the bottleneck is evidence, disclosure, or physical verification.

Assign one cross-folder compare-and-contrast: same concept, two source cultures (lab blog vs policy center vs engineering journalism). Students should leave able to explain *why* the framings differ—not only recite facts from one page.


## From trophy case to decision memo

A useful seminar assignment: take any popular leaderboard and write a one-page decision memo answering “so what?” If the memo cannot name a hiring, procurement, open-weight, or timeline decision the score changes, the suite failed Epoch’s test. MirrorCode and agent-run businesses exist to make that “so what?” painful and concrete—packages and cafés do not grade like multiple choice.

## Takeaways

- Start benchmark design from the **decision** you need to make.
- Mix narrow verified tasks with **job-shaped** and **research-shaped** evals.
- Publish methodology so others can stress-test whether your suite actually tracks the question.
- Leaderboard wins that answer no economic question are decoration.
- Open/closed lags and $/threshold curves belong in the same syllabus as accuracy charts.
