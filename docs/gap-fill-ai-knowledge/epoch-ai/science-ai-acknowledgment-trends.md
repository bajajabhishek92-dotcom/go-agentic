# Biology and Math Preprints: AI Acknowledgment Trends

Scientific fields are adopting AI at different tempos—and they **disclose** that adoption unevenly. Epoch’s preprint analyses turn acknowledgment language into a measurable diffusion signal: not a perfect census of use, but a public lower bound on how normal AI has become to *declare*.

## Biology: LLMs catching specialized AI tools

On bioRxiv, the share of preprints that **acknowledge LLM use** has grown exponentially since 2023. Within about three years, LLM acknowledgments approached those of **specialized AI tools** (Epoch cites roughly **17% vs 19%** in a recent cross-section)—tools whose acknowledgments had been climbing more steadily since 2021.

Teach the distinction:

- **Specialized AI** — domain models (structure prediction, imaging, genomics pipelines) that often appear in methods sections as scientific instruments.
- **General LLMs** — writing, coding, literature search, and analysis assistance that may once have felt too “editorial” to mention.

Near-parity in *acknowledgment rates* does not mean equal scientific contribution; it means LLMs have become **normal enough to declare** almost as often as classic bio-AI tools. That cultural shift matters for journals drafting disclosure policies and for historians tracking when generative AI entered wet-lab-adjacent workflows.

## Math: a sharp 2026 jump

In mathematics preprints, Epoch reports acknowledged AI use rising from about **4% in April to 25% in August** (same-year window in their data insight). That is a steeper short-run climb than biology’s multi-year LLM curve—suggesting field norms and tooling (proof assistants, coding agents, writing help) can shift disclosure culture quickly. Month-to-month jumps warn against extrapolating from a single snapshot; they also warn editors that templates and peer expectations can move faster than formal policy.

## How to use these stats without abusing them

- Treat acknowledgments as a **lower bound** on use; many authors still omit AI help for stigma, habit, or fear of desk rejection.
- Separate “AI for research substance” from “AI for prose” when papers draw the distinction—governance and credit differ.
- Watch whether journals and preprint servers **standardize disclosure templates**—measured rates can jump overnight without a true usage jump.
- Pair with integrity concerns (AI-written papers, detector failure modes): acknowledgment culture and detection are twin governance problems, not substitutes.
- Compare fields: biology’s specialized-tool baseline vs math’s sudden LLM/agent wave shows **different diffusion channels**.

## Classroom and practice checklist

Use this piece as a concept explainer, not a news brief. When teaching or briefing:

- Restate the **core mechanism** in one sentence before citing any organization or product name.
- Separate **what is measured** from **what is claimed**—numbers, thresholds, and evaluation designs are the durable content.
- Map at least one **failure mode** (silent fallback, overclaim, approval fatigue, distribution shift, or missing evidence chain).
- Name the **operator decision** the idea changes: release gating, procurement, curriculum design, facility build, or legal scope.
- Leave students with a **verification habit**: which primary source, diagnostic, or audit would falsify the headline?

These habits travel across geopolitics, privacy law, compute economics, agent harnesses, and applied science stacks alike.

## Takeaways

- Diffusion in science is visible first as **language in papers**, not only as new methods sections.
- Compare **LLM vs specialized tool** acknowledgments to see whether AI is a writing layer or a methods layer.
- Rapid month-to-month jumps (math) demand continuous measurement, not annual anecdotes.
- Disclosure policy design will reshape the metric—instrument the change consciously.
