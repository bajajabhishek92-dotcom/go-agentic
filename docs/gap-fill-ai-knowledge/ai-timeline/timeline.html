How AI got here

Five operating regimes—not a product-launch list. Scan the chips, skim takeaways, leave with the arc in about three minutes.

~3 min cold read · Markdown hub: index.md · Evidence: Stanford AI Index · Epoch · Anthropic
≤2011 Features 2012–16 Deep learning 2017–21 Foundations 2022–23 Assistants 2024→ Agents

≤2011

Features & narrow ML

What happened

  • Production AI = rules, search, classical ML, hand-built features.
  • Competent but single-purpose: spam, ranking, speech, fraud.
  • Users met AI as autocomplete—not a collaborator.

Why it mattered

Progress was real, but every new domain needed specialists and a new stack.

Key players

Academic ML labs · search/ads teams · IBM · classical vision/speech groups

Takeaway: AI was infrastructure you didn’t chat with.

~2012–2016

Deep learning

What happened

  • Neural nets learn representations end-to-end with data + GPUs.
  • Vision, then speech and language, leave handcrafted features.
  • “Scale the model and the data” becomes an industrial strategy.

Why it mattered

Scarce skill shifts from feature craft toward training scale—products stay mostly single-task.

Key players

Toronto / NYU / Stanford / Montreal · NVIDIA · Google Brain · DeepMind · FAIR · ImageNet

Takeaway: Representation learning went mainstream; generality did not—yet.

~2017–2021

Transformers & foundation models

What happened

  • Transformers make large-scale language (then multimodal) pretraining practical.
  • One foundation model + prompting/fine-tuning replaces train-from-scratch per task.
  • Broad benchmarks appear; capability surprise becomes normal.

Why it mattered

Unit of progress becomes “a general model plus adaptation.”

Key players

Google (Transformer, BERT) · OpenAI (GPT) · Hugging Face · DeepMind · Meta research

Takeaway: Generality became the product claim.

~2022–2023

Assistants

What happened

  • Chat UX turns models into consumer collaborators.
  • Writing, coding, Q&A habits scale; near-daily use jumps in polling.
  • Inference price for a fixed capability level plunges.

Why it mattered

Competition shifts to product loops: latency, trust, cost, refusals.

Key players

OpenAI · Anthropic · Google · Microsoft · open-weight challengers

Takeaway: People started treating models as collaborators, not APIs.

2024 → · YOU ARE HERE

Agents & everyday adoption

What happened

  • Systems call tools, modify state, and pursue multi-step goals.
  • Harnesses (orchestration, sandboxes, escalation) sit beside the weights.
  • Adoption leaves novelty: enterprise uptake, regulated devices, science workflows; open–closed gaps shrink to months.

Why it mattered

Failure modes, economics, and evaluation all change once AI acts. Governance lags.

Key players

Anthropic · OpenAI · Google DeepMind · hyperscalers & NVIDIA · open-weight labs · METR / Epoch · regulators

Takeaway: The scarce skill is systems design + outcome eval—not just prompting.

Read without mythology

  1. Eras overlap — classical ML still runs fraud; assistants and agents ship together.
  2. Inflection ≠ invention — eras mark when defaults and investment shifted.
  3. Frontiers are plural — lead times at the top now last months, not years.
